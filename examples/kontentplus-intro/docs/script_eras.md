# KontentPlus — "Knowing the customer" · scene-by-scene narrative (v2, ≈75 s, 16:9)

**Premise.** Marketing has always been one job: know the customer and act on it. Only the tools changed. Five eras show
the job growing, then fragmenting; then today, an AI operator runs it. One visual motif carries every cut: **a hand doing
the work** (offering cloth → setting type → pinning a board → pressing a remote → scrolling a feed → two keystrokes).

**Voice.** A dry, warm historian. Every era names its actor (the merchant, the printed page, agencies, brands, the platforms).
Decades, not years.

**Product framing.** KontentPlus is an AI operator for marketing operations, for any company: it builds a living brain from
your own files, runs the whole operation across every channel, and compounds by learning from every edit and every result.

## Act 1 — The job gets harder (0:00–0:36)

### Scene 1 · The marketplace (0:00–0:07)
**Picture.** An ancient market at dawn: spice baskets, dyed cloth, backs and silhouettes. A merchant's weathered hands lift a length of blue cloth toward a customer; she leans to a neighbour and word passes stall to stall; a small crowd gathers, coins change hands. Warm earth tones, early-cinema softness, shown in a slightly narrowed window.
**On screen.** `THE MARKETPLACE`
**VO.** "Since the first market, the merchant knew every buyer by face. And word travelled by mouth."
**Sound.** Market murmur, a bell, fabric, coins. A lone melody, hand percussion.

### Scene 2 · The 1920s · The printed page (0:07–0:14)
**Picture.** A letterpress shop, dust in window light, the compositor a silhouette. Hands set lead type, ink the forme, pull the lever; a printed advertisement lifts to the camera. Sepia, silent-era grain, 4:3.
**On screen.** `1920s`
**VO.** "In the 1920s, the printed page carried the message to thousands."
**Sound.** Type clacking, roller hiss, press thump. Ragtime piano takes the melody.

### Scene 3 · The 1960s · The agency (0:14–0:21)
**Picture.** Madison Avenue: venetian-blind light, cigarette haze. A cuffed hand pins a storyboard to a corkboard; men in ties lean in; a projector throws a smiling toaster ad on the wall. Kodachrome, a wider rounded frame.
**On screen.** `1960s`
**VO.** "In the 1960s, agencies sold by demographic. One message, for everyone."
**Sound.** Pin press, murmur, projector clack. Cool jazz.

### Scene 4 · The 1990s · TV and the mailbox (0:21–0:28)
**Picture.** A thumb on a chunky remote; a family lit by a CRT; into the kitchen: catalogues, a fax curling out and torn off. VHS softness, 4:3.
**On screen.** `1990s`
**VO.** "In the 1990s, brands knew you by zip code. And filled your mailbox."
**Sound.** Remote click, muffled TV, fax whine. Synth-pop.

### Scene 5 · The 2010s · The feed (0:28–0:36)
**Picture.** Over a marketer's shoulder: a thumb scrolls an endless feed; three monitors of dashboards, tabs, red badges; popups over popups until the frame washes to white. Cold blue, handheld, full 16:9.

**VO.** "In the 2010s, the platforms knew everything. Marketing became ten dashboards, and no time."
